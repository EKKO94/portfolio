Preparing data

Context

Making a technical setup easier to follow.

AI Trainer, formerly ML Trainer, is the part of Sorba’s platform where people prepare and version their data, create projects to analyse it and build models. Everything starts with a dataset, so this case follows that part of the product.

The project took about four months, and I was its only product designer. Parts of the product are under NDA, and many early files were not saved. This case presents a selection of surviving Figma screens with a retrospective account of the work.

In the product Live
The new dataset forms are live in the Sorba demo, available after signing up and downloading it. The redesigned overview, dataset list, versions, explorer and warning dialogs remain proposals.
Redesign Proposal
A new overview, dataset list, version pages, explorer and warning dialogs. These are designs, not yet in the product.

My contribution

  • Redesigned many of the forms and created new forms and flows, including the dataset forms now in the demo: CSV and XLSX import, aggregation, per-field settings and a hot-data source.
  • Defined a new visual style: stronger contrast, more generous spacing and colour applied consistently to every element.
  • Proposed a redesign of the dataset list, versions, explorer and warnings, and a new overview.

Working with developers helped me understand the system and its technical limits. It could not tell me whether people would understand the interface, so I treat that as a question to test, not a finding. I had no direct access to customers. Feedback came only from developers testing the work and from the e-commerce team. The surviving files do not provide a complete iteration history. Labels such as “OLD” and “v1” do not establish the sequence or approval status of each design.

Overview · proposal

A home that shows the state of your data.

The earlier overview duplicates navigation and labels the Projects card “Datasets”; its Predictions card also uses dataset actions. My proposal removes the extra navigation column, gives each object its own labels and actions, and places status summaries below.

Three decisions in the proposal

Use the width for the work Proposal
The second “Quick Navigation” column is removed; the main product rail stays.
Match labels to objects Proposal
The Projects card is named Projects, and Predictions has “New Prediction” and “All Predictions” actions.
Separate entry points from status Proposal
Dataset, project and prediction cards sit above status summaries, templates, favorites and recent activity.

The proposed overview has more content and is taller. I am not claiming it reduces scrolling or time to task.

Visual style

One look for every element.

Alongside the flows, I defined a new visual style for AI Trainer, with stronger contrast, more generous spacing and consistent colour roles across interface elements.

You can see it in the proposed overview above: solid primary buttons, icon tiles on the summary cards, donut charts with counts in their legends, and a table for activity. The earlier views use outlined buttons and simpler cards.

Dataset forms · live

Forms that handle real files.

An earlier saved form shows a CSV field labeled “Only .zip files are allowed”. The new form accepts CSV or XLSX by drag and drop, then asks for the format, the date field, date format, time zone and how to aggregate by time interval.

The new dataset forms are live in the Sorba demo, which people can sign up for and download to try.

Settings for every field.

I kept each field’s operation beside its name, with options to replace or discard values and controls labeled LL and HL. Source settings, field operations and rules sit within one configuration form. Keeping them together makes the configuration visible, but the form remains long; I would test where people need more guidance or progressive disclosure.

Field preferences can be saved and imported; synthetic tags and advanced rules have separate tables with Export and Import controls. Open the complete form ↗ (image, opens in a new tab)

Hot data source · live

Two ways to pick live data.

The hot-data source can be set up with a query in an editor, or by choosing measurements from a list. A view toggle switches between the two, so people who write queries and people who prefer a table use the same form.

Rules and recovery · saved designs

Make the rule visible. Make mistakes clear.

A rule is more than a dropdown choice. The builder keeps its field, operation and value together, lists each condition and shows the resulting expression before saving, so the configuration can be inspected.

The saved designs also cover an existing version identifier and leaving a form with unsaved work. The exit confirmation is a proposal; the release status of the rule builder and inline version-conflict state is not confirmed.

The proposed exit dialog uses “Cancel” for staying in the form; I would test a clearer label such as “Keep editing”. The saved forms show a Preview action, but no preview-result screen is included in this case.

Dataset module · proposal

A clearer list of what you have.

The earlier dataset cards already show a status icon and a favorite star. My proposal adds a numeric version count and a selection checkbox, while retaining the name and date.

Versions · proposal

The dataset stays in view as versions change.

A dataset and its versions are different objects. In the proposal, the selected dataset stays in the list while its versions appear beside it. Version, source and creation date help tell entries apart, and “New Version” and “Explore” make the next actions explicit.

Dataset health · proposal

Status you can read before you open it.

A dataset holds versions, and a version can fail on its own. The card shows a count of versions, and its colour and tooltip say what the count means: all good, one invalid version but still usable, or every version with issues.

Version properties show which extra processing is marked APPLIED or NOT APPLIED.

Explorer and warnings · proposal

Look before you train, think before you delete.

The proposed explorer keeps the selected dataset beside summary charts and analysis tabs. The proposed delete-version dialog warns that deletion cannot be undone and may affect models or processes using that version.

The charts in these designs use sample data.

How I worked

Working quickly with internal feedback.

I designed new and revised forms, flows and a visual style, with feedback from developers testing the work and from the e-commerce team. The surviving screens show variants, but they do not provide a complete record linking feedback to individual changes. I had no direct access to customers.

This account combines my recollection with the surviving Figma screens.

Design outcome & reflection

Dataset forms in the demo. Module redesign proposed.

The new dataset forms are part of the Sorba demo. The redesigned overview, list, versions, explorer and warnings remain proposals, and the product still shows the earlier views there.

The designs show how I structure a technical product and make its decisions visible: new forms, rules, versions, status and recovery states. They do not establish fewer errors, faster completion, higher adoption or successful usability with end users, and I do not have usage data.

What I would test next

  1. Choose and configure a source

    Can someone pick the right source and set the date and time options without help?

  2. Explain a field operation or a rule

    Can they describe the effect of an operation or a combined rule before saving?

  3. Distinguish datasets, versions and recovery states

    Can someone identify the selected dataset and version, recover from a naming conflict, and leave a form without losing work by accident?

I would observe task completion, errors, requests for help and how people read the saved configuration. Before implementation, I would also review contrast, form length, disclosure of advanced settings and whether status remains understandable without colour or hover. Developer discussion was technical input, not usability validation.

What I would do differently

  1. Write it down as I go

    Keep a short decision log per screen: the problem, the variants and what changed after feedback. That is the part this case cannot show.

  2. Test with the people who prepare data

    Watch someone import a file and create a dataset version; note where they stop or guess.

  3. Review the proposal in stages

    Review the proposed list and version-status views with the team, then test them before deciding what to build next.
